Backing Up Truck and Trailer

DS Home Computing Ltd · SA03 SECOR · For internal use

Policy Overview

This policy outlines the safe procedures for vehicle and trailer backing operations for all employees of DS Home Computing Ltd. (DSHC). It aims to minimize the risk of accidents, injuries, and property damage when backing vehicles and trailers, while ensuring compliance with national and provincial safety regulations.

Scope

This policy applies to all employees who operate company vehicles or trailers as part of their duties, whether in Alberta, British Columbia, Quebec, or other areas where DSHC operates.

Legal Compliance

DS Home Computing Ltd. will adhere to the following laws and regulations to ensure safety when backing vehicles and trailers:

Canada Labour Code (CLC) and Occupational Health and Safety Regulations (OHS) for federal operations

Alberta Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Code

British Columbia Workers Compensation Act and the Occupational Health and Safety Regulation (OHSR)

Quebec’s Act Respecting Occupational Health and Safety and related regulations

Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations (TDG), when applicable

Motor Vehicle Act (MVA) and Traffic Safety Act (TSA) for operational safety

This policy integrates these regulations to ensure a consistent, safety-first approach to vehicle and trailer backing operations.

General Guidelines

Pre-Backing Preparation

Ensure the vehicle is properly secured, with the engine off, parking brake engaged, and transmission in the proper gear.

Inspect the area around the vehicle for any potential hazards, including pedestrians, obstacles, or low-hanging overhead objects.

Verify that mirrors, cameras, or other visibility aids are functional and properly adjusted. Ensure that the backing camera, if present, is clean and free of obstruction.

If backing with a trailer, ensure the trailer is properly hitched and the connection is secure.

Spotter Requirement

A trained spotter must be present whenever possible during backing operations, especially in tight or high-traffic areas. The spotter should be positioned where they can see both the driver and the intended path of the vehicle.

The spotter must use hand signals or walkie-talkies to communicate with the driver clearly.

In situations where a spotter is unavailable, drivers must use mirrors and cameras to the best of their ability and drive at a slow speed, making small adjustments.

Vehicle Backing Procedure

Before backing, ensure you have a clear line of sight in all directions, including via mirrors, cameras, or a spotter.

Always back in a slow, controlled manner. Maintain full control of the vehicle, using small steering movements.

If the vehicle has a trailer, always check that the trailer is aligned and there is sufficient clearance for all turns and backing maneuvers.

Never back more than necessary. If the backing maneuver requires several adjustments, proceed slowly and cautiously.

Use a "360-degree walkaround" to inspect the area before performing any backing. Ensure there are no obstacles or hazards in the immediate area.

Trailer-Specific Backing Procedures

When backing with a trailer, always ensure the trailer is properly aligned with the tow vehicle before initiating movement.

Use the "S" or "Z" method when backing, where the driver adjusts the steering to keep the trailer aligned with the vehicle.

When possible, perform trailer backing in an open, spacious area to minimize risk of collisions.

Check trailer lights and brake functionality before embarking on any trip.

Vehicle and Trailer Parking

When parking vehicles with trailers, ensure that the vehicle and trailer are parked in a safe and designated area, away from pedestrian paths or high-traffic zones.

Always engage the parking brake and ensure the vehicle is in “Park” (or equivalent gear for manual vehicles).

If parking a trailer, ensure that wheel chocks are used where applicable, especially on inclined surfaces.

Employee Training and Competency

Training Requirements

All employees required to operate vehicles and trailers must complete a safety training course that includes:

Safe vehicle and trailer backing techniques

Proper use of mirrors, cameras, and spotters

Emergency response protocols in case of an accident

Workplace hazard recognition and risk mitigation strategies

Vehicle inspection procedures

Specific regulations governing vehicle operation in each province (Alberta, British Columbia, Quebec)

Certification

Employees who operate company vehicles and trailers will be required to pass a competency test that evaluates their ability to safely back a vehicle and trailer. Refresher training will be conducted on an annual basis or when safety concerns arise.

Ongoing Safety Reviews

Drivers will be evaluated for compliance with this policy through regular safety audits and incident reviews. Any employee found to be in violation of the backing procedures may face corrective action, including retraining or suspension from vehicle use.

Risk Assessment and Incident Reporting

Risk Assessment

Prior to any backing operation, employees must assess the risk level by considering the area, vehicle type, load type, and weather conditions. Higher-risk operations, such as in busy parking lots, construction zones, or poor weather conditions, may require additional precautions, such as the use of additional spotters or restricted movement.

Incident Reporting

Any incident or near-miss involving vehicle or trailer backing must be reported immediately to the Safety Officer or supervisor.

An investigation will be conducted for each report, and corrective action will be taken to prevent reoccurrence.

Equipment Maintenance and Inspection

Regular Inspections

All vehicles and trailers must undergo routine safety inspections at intervals not exceeding 12 months, or more frequently if required by local laws.

Inspections must cover the functionality of all critical components, including braking systems, lights, mirrors, back-up cameras, and coupling mechanisms for trailers.

Maintenance

Any damage or malfunction identified during inspection must be addressed immediately. Employees are not permitted to operate vehicles or trailers that do not meet safety standards.

Policy Enforcement

Failure to adhere to the Vehicle and Trailer Backing Safety Policy may result in disciplinary actions, including retraining, suspension, or termination, depending on the severity of the violation.

Conclusion

This policy has been developed to ensure the safety of our employees, clients, and the public when backing vehicles or trailers. By following these procedures and complying with all relevant laws and regulations, DS Home Computing Ltd. will maintain a high standard of operational safety and reduce the risk of accidents.
