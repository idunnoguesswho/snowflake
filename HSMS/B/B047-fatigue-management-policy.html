Fatigue Management Policy

DS Home Computing Ltd · SA03 SECOR · For internal use

Policy brief & purpose

DS Home Computing Ltd recognizes fatigue as a form of impairment that can reduce alertness, judgment, reaction time, communication, and safe work performance. This policy establishes requirements to identify, report, prevent, and control fatigue-related risk before it results in injury, collision, property damage, poor workmanship, or other loss.

This policy supports the Health and Safety Management System (HSMS) commitment to hazard assessment, hazard control, fit for work, employee participation, shared responsibility, training, confidentiality, and continuous improvement.

Scope

This policy applies to all employees and subcontractors whose work may be affected by fatigue, including driving, field work, travel, work alone, after-hours response, extended shifts, overtime, emergency work, office work, and safety-sensitive tasks.

Policy elements

  • Workers must report fit for work and must not perform work or drive for work when fatigue may create a risk to themselves, co-workers, clients, the public, or Company property.
  • Supervisors must consider fatigue hazards during work planning, hazard assessments, scheduling, journey planning, and incident investigations.
  • Workers are expected to report fatigue early, without fear of retaliation, so that controls can be put in place before work becomes unsafe.
  • Fatigue controls may include rest breaks, task rotation, schedule changes, reduced driving, split travel, lodging, alternate drivers, delay of non-urgent work, reassignment, or removal from safety-sensitive tasks.
  • Workers do not have to disclose private medical information or the personal cause of fatigue unless required to support a specific accommodation, legal requirement, or safety assessment.
  • No worker may instruct, pressure, or permit another worker to continue work or driving where fatigue creates an unreasonable risk.

Definitions

TermPolicy meaning
FatigueA state of tiredness, sleepiness, reduced alertness, or reduced capacity that may be caused by lack of sleep, long hours, physical or mental workload, travel, illness, medication, stress, or other factors.
Safety-sensitive taskWork where impairment, distraction, or reduced alertness could reasonably result in injury, collision, damage, environmental impact, or serious operational error.
Fit for workPhysically and psychologically capable of performing assigned duties safely and competently.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibilities

Workers / DriversFollow the policy, remain fit for work, complete required forms and inspections honestly, report hazards or fatigue, obey traffic laws, and refuse or stop unsafe work when required.
Human Resources / AdministrationMaintain confidential records, support training, assist with accommodation or employment questions, and coordinate documentation, deductions, payroll or corrective action where applicable.

Fatigue risk factors

  • Less than adequate sleep or poor sleep quality.
  • Extended work hours, overtime, on-call work, night work, early starts, long commutes, or long-distance driving.
  • Physically demanding work, repetitive work, high mental workload, stress, illness, medication, heat, cold, or monotonous driving.
  • Working alone, remote work, poor lighting, poor weather, or work after emergency response.

Minimum controls

  • Plan work to avoid unnecessary extended shifts and high-risk driving after long work periods.
  • Schedule breaks before workers become unsafe, especially for driving and repetitive or safety-sensitive tasks.
  • Use journey management for long trips, poor weather, remote travel, unfamiliar routes, or travel after extended work.
  • Stop the task or driving when fatigue signs are observed and contact a supervisor for direction.
  • Document fatigue-related incidents, near misses, and controls for review.

Reporting, review, and corrective action

Employees and subcontractors must promptly report unsafe conditions, vehicle or equipment defects, fatigue concerns, journey concerns, incidents, near misses, injuries, damage, fines, tickets, collisions, and policy breaches to their supervisor, Project Manager, Human Resources Manager, or owner.

The Company will review reported concerns, implement corrective actions where required, and document outcomes. Corrective action may include coaching, retraining, revised schedules, removal from driving duties, loss of vehicle privileges, written warning, suspension, termination, or contractor removal depending on the circumstances.

Confidentiality and records

Records created under this policy will be kept only as long as required for safety, employment, payroll, accounting, insurance, vehicle maintenance, investigation, legal, or business purposes. Personal employee information will be collected, used, disclosed, stored, and destroyed in a reasonable manner and shared only with persons who have a legitimate need to know or where required by law.

Training, communication, and review

This policy will be communicated during orientation, before a worker is assigned affected duties, when a change in work creates additional risk, and when changes occur. Supervisors and managers are responsible for applying the policy consistently. This policy will be reviewed when legislation changes, after a significant incident or audit finding, when operations change, or at least every three (3) years as part of HSMS administration.
