Journey Management Policy

DS Home Computing Ltd · SA03 SECOR · For internal use

Policy brief & purpose

DS Home Computing Ltd uses journey management to plan, authorize, monitor, and close out work-related travel where driving or travel conditions may create elevated risk. The goal is to prevent driving incidents by identifying hazards before departure and putting reasonable controls in place.

This policy supports the Health and Safety Management System (HSMS) commitment to hazard assessment, hazard control, fit for work, employee participation, shared responsibility, training, confidentiality, and continuous improvement.

Scope

This policy applies to work-related travel by employees and subcontractors, including long-distance trips, remote travel, unfamiliar routes, travel during poor weather, travel outside normal hours, travel after long work periods, travel with trailers or loads, and travel to client or project sites.

Policy elements

  • Journey management must be used when a trip has elevated risk because of distance, duration, weather, road conditions, fatigue, remote location, working alone, unfamiliar route, load, towing, client requirement, or supervisor direction.
  • Trips requiring journey management must be planned and approved before departure where reasonably practicable.
  • Drivers must complete the required pre-trip information, verify the vehicle is suitable, complete the 360 before driving check, confirm communication methods, and identify rest, fuel, and emergency plans.
  • A check-in process must be used for higher-risk journeys. Missed check-ins must be escalated according to the plan.
  • Supervisors may delay, cancel, reroute, split, or modify travel where risk is not adequately controlled.
  • Drivers must stop and contact a supervisor if conditions change during the journey, including fatigue, weather, road closures, vehicle problems, communication loss, or unexpected delays.

Definitions

When a journey plan is required

  • Driving more than two hours one way or outside the local work area.
  • Travel before 6:00 a.m., after 8:00 p.m., overnight, or after extended work hours.
  • Poor weather, wildfire smoke, poor visibility, icy roads, road closures, construction, or remote conditions are expected.
  • Travel includes working alone, unfamiliar destinations, rural or remote roads, towing, heavy loads, or multiple stops.
  • Client, site, supervisor, or project requirements call for a journey plan.

Journey planning controls

  • Check road, weather, wildfire smoke, traffic, fuel or charging, communications, and route conditions before departure.
  • Set realistic travel times and include breaks, meals, fueling, and rest stops.
  • Use approved routes where possible and avoid high-risk shortcuts.
  • Confirm the vehicle, tires, lights, wipers, emergency kit, load securement, and communication tools are suitable.
  • Identify a check-in person, check-in times, overdue response time, and emergency escalation contact.

Journey management form instructions

  • Complete the attached Journey Management Form for trips that meet this policy or supervisor direction.
  • Submit the plan to the supervisor or designated check-in person before departure.
  • Update the plan if hazards or timing change.
  • Close the plan when the worker safely arrives or returns.

Reporting, review, and corrective action

Employees and subcontractors must promptly report unsafe conditions, vehicle or equipment defects, fatigue concerns, journey concerns, incidents, near misses, injuries, damage, fines, tickets, collisions, and policy breaches to their supervisor, Project Manager, Human Resources Manager, or owner.

The Company will review reported concerns, implement corrective actions where required, and document outcomes. Corrective action may include coaching, retraining, revised schedules, removal from driving duties, loss of vehicle privileges, written warning, suspension, termination, or contractor removal depending on the circumstances.

Confidentiality and records

Records created under this policy will be kept only as long as required for safety, employment, payroll, accounting, insurance, vehicle maintenance, investigation, legal, or business purposes. Personal employee information will be collected, used, disclosed, stored, and destroyed in a reasonable manner and shared only with persons who have a legitimate need to know or where required by law.

Training, communication, and review

This policy will be communicated during orientation, before a worker is assigned affected duties, when a change in work creates additional risk, and when changes occur. Supervisors and managers are responsible for applying the policy consistently. This policy will be reviewed when legislation changes, after a significant incident or audit finding, when operations change, or at least every three (3) years as part of HSMS administration.
