360 Before Driving Policy

DS Home Computing Ltd · SA03 SECOR · For internal use

Policy brief & purpose

DS Home Computing Ltd requires drivers to complete a 360-degree walk-around check before driving to identify hazards around the vehicle, confirm the vehicle appears safe to move, and prevent preventable incidents such as backing collisions, struck-by incidents, property damage, flat tires, fluid leaks, unsecured loads, or forgotten equipment.

This policy supports the Health and Safety Management System (HSMS) commitment to hazard assessment, hazard control, fit for work, employee participation, shared responsibility, training, confidentiality, and continuous improvement.

Scope

This policy applies to all employees, subcontractors, supervisors, managers, and authorized drivers who move, start, reverse, park, or operate Company-owned, leased, rented, client-provided, or personal vehicles for Company business.

Policy elements

  • A 360-degree walk-around must be completed before the first movement of a vehicle, after each stop where conditions around the vehicle may have changed, before reversing where practical, after loading or unloading, and whenever the driver is unsure whether the area around the vehicle is clear.
  • Drivers must look high, low, front, rear, sides, under/near the vehicle, around tires, behind the vehicle, and above the vehicle for people, children, animals, equipment, tools, cords, leaks, damage, obstructions, overhead hazards, and changing conditions.
  • Drivers must correct hazards before moving the vehicle or contact a supervisor for direction if the hazard cannot be safely corrected.
  • Drivers must not rely only on mirrors, cameras, sensors, alarms, or another person when a walk-around can reasonably be completed.
  • When a spotter is used, the driver and spotter must agree on signals, maintain visibility, stop if the spotter is lost from view, and never position the spotter in a crush zone.
  • The 360 check does not replace required vehicle inspections, journey planning, commercial vehicle requirements, or site-specific rules.

Required 360 walk-around steps

  • Start at the driver door and walk fully around the vehicle before entering or moving it.
  • Check for people, children, visitors, animals, bikes, tools, cords, cones, bins, curbs, ditches, overhead objects, and nearby traffic.
  • Check tires, obvious fluid leaks, lights, mirrors, windows, licence plate, load securement, trailer connection, and visible damage.
  • Look behind and under the vehicle where practical and safe.
  • Remove hazards, secure items, or get assistance before moving.
  • Recheck if delayed, distracted, or if people or conditions change before movement.

Backing and parking expectations

  • Avoid backing where a safer option exists. Choose pull-through parking when practical.
  • Back into parking spaces on arrival when safe to do so, rather than backing out later into unknown conditions.
  • Use a spotter for tight, congested, blind, or high-risk backing where available.
  • Stop immediately if the driver loses sight of the spotter, a person enters the area, an alarm sounds, or the driver is uncertain.

Documentation

  • Daily documentation may be required by supervisor, client, project, vehicle type, or journey plan.
  • Drivers must document defects, damage, hazards, or near misses discovered during the 360 check.
  • Supervisors may conduct field observations to verify the 360 habit is being used.

Reporting, review, and corrective action

Employees and subcontractors must promptly report unsafe conditions, vehicle or equipment defects, fatigue concerns, journey concerns, incidents, near misses, injuries, damage, fines, tickets, collisions, and policy breaches to their supervisor, Project Manager, Human Resources Manager, or owner.

The Company will review reported concerns, implement corrective actions where required, and document outcomes. Corrective action may include coaching, retraining, revised schedules, removal from driving duties, loss of vehicle privileges, written warning, suspension, termination, or contractor removal depending on the circumstances.

Confidentiality and records

Records created under this policy will be kept only as long as required for safety, employment, payroll, accounting, insurance, vehicle maintenance, investigation, legal, or business purposes. Personal employee information will be collected, used, disclosed, stored, and destroyed in a reasonable manner and shared only with persons who have a legitimate need to know or where required by law.

Training, communication, and review

This policy will be communicated during orientation, before a worker is assigned affected duties, when a change in work creates additional risk, and when changes occur. Supervisors and managers are responsible for applying the policy consistently. This policy will be reviewed when legislation changes, after a significant incident or audit finding, when operations change, or at least every three (3) years as part of HSMS administration.
