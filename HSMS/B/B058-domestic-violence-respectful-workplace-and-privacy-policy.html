Domestic Violence Respectful Workplace and Privacy Policy

DS Home Computing Ltd · SA03 SECOR · For internal use

Policy brief & purpose

DS Home Computing Ltd is committed to a physically and psychologically safe workplace where domestic violence hazards are addressed, employees and subcontractors are treated respectfully, and personal information is protected. This policy supports the Health and Safety Management System (HSMS), including the Management Commitment, Hazard Assessment, Violence and Harassment Prevention, Training and Communication, and Enforcement and Corrective Action sections.

This policy is intended to meet or support the requirements of Alberta occupational health and safety legislation, Alberta human rights legislation, and Alberta private-sector privacy legislation. Where this policy conflicts with legislation, the legislation will apply.

Scope

This policy applies to all DS Home Computing Ltd employees, subcontractors, managers, supervisors, owners, visitors, clients, and any other person present at a DS Home Computing Ltd work site or participating in work-related activities.

For this policy, the workplace includes offices, client sites, project sites, company vehicles, rented units or accommodations arranged or paid for by the Company, remote work, work-related travel, training, meetings, electronic communications, and any work-related social or business activity.

Policy elements

From the effective date of this policy forward, DS Home Computing Ltd will not tolerate workplace violence, domestic violence that affects the workplace, harassment, discrimination, bullying, retaliation, or misuse of personal information.

  • Every worker has the right to a respectful workplace free from violence and harassment.
  • Every worker must refrain from causing, participating in, encouraging, ignoring, or retaliating against reports of violence, harassment, discrimination, or privacy breaches.
  • Managers and supervisors must take reports seriously, respond promptly, control hazards, protect confidentiality, and document actions taken.
  • Personal information must be collected, used, disclosed, retained, and destroyed only for reasonable, authorized business, safety, employment, legal, or benefit-administration purposes.
  • Reasonable management actions, including coaching, performance management, work assignment, discipline, scheduling, or enforcement of safety rules, are not harassment when carried out respectfully and in good faith.

Definitions

Domestic Violence in the Workplace

Domestic violence is a workplace hazard when the Company is aware that a worker is, or is likely to be, exposed to domestic violence at a work site. The Company will take reasonable precautions to protect the affected worker and any other person at the work site who may be affected.

Workers are encouraged to tell a supervisor, manager, owner, or Human Resources representative if domestic violence may affect their safety at work. A worker does not need to share unnecessary personal details to request help. Information will be limited to those who need it to protect safety, investigate, take corrective action, comply with law, or provide support.

When a domestic violence concern is reported or becomes known, the Company may, as appropriate and in consultation with the affected worker where safe and reasonably practicable:

  • Complete or update a hazard assessment and identify reasonable controls.
  • Develop a confidential workplace safety plan focused on work-related risks.
  • Change work arrangements, communication procedures, parking or entry arrangements, work location, or scheduling where reasonable.
  • Inform only those workers who need to know about a specific or general threat so they can protect themselves and others.
  • Preserve evidence and records only as necessary for safety, investigation, employment, or legal purposes.
  • Contact emergency services or law enforcement where there is immediate danger or where required by law.

A worker who has an injury or adverse symptom resulting from workplace violence or harassment will be advised to consult a health professional of the worker’s choice for treatment or referral. Requests for leave, schedule changes, accommodation, or other supports will be assessed on a case-by-case basis in accordance with applicable employment, human rights, safety, and privacy requirements.

Respectful Workplace

DS Home Computing Ltd expects all workers and worksite parties to act professionally and respectfully. Prohibited conduct includes, but is not limited to:

  • Threatening, intimidating, aggressive, or violent behaviour.
  • Bullying, yelling, humiliation, repeated insults, spreading malicious rumours, social exclusion intended to harm, or persistent undermining.
  • Discriminatory comments, jokes, images, slurs, gestures, or conduct connected to a protected ground under Alberta human rights legislation.
  • Unwelcome sexual comments, advances, messages, images, touching, requests, or conduct.
  • Retaliation against a person who reports a concern, participates in an investigation, requests accommodation, refuses unsafe work, or supports another worker.
  • Knowingly making a false or malicious complaint.

Workers who feel safe and comfortable may tell the person to stop the behaviour. Workers are not required to confront the person before reporting. Reports may be made to a supervisor, manager, owner, or Human Resources representative the worker feels comfortable approaching.

Personal Privacy in the Workplace

DS Home Computing Ltd respects employee privacy while recognizing that the Company must collect, use, disclose, store, and retain certain personal employee information to establish, manage, or end employment or subcontractor relationships, administer payroll and benefits, meet safety obligations, comply with law, protect Company property, and manage the workplace.

The Company will:

  • Collect only information that is reasonably necessary for an authorized business, safety, employment, or legal purpose.
  • Provide reasonable notice of the purpose for collecting, using, or disclosing current employees’ personal employee information, unless an exception applies by law.
  • Limit access to personal information to persons who need it to perform their role.
  • Store records securely in paper or electronic form and protect them from unauthorized access, loss, misuse, or disclosure.
  • Use monitoring, device review, GPS, cameras, email review, internet review, or access logs only where reasonable, authorized, and connected to a legitimate business, safety, security, legal, or investigation purpose.
  • Retain records only as long as needed for business, safety, employment, legal, insurance, WCB, audit, or statutory reasons, and securely dispose of records when no longer required.
  • Correct or update inaccurate personal information where appropriate and practicable.
  • Require subcontractors or service providers who handle personal information for the Company to protect that information and use it only for authorized purposes.

Workers must protect the privacy of other workers, clients, and Company records. Workers must not access, photograph, copy, share, post, remove, or disclose personal information unless authorized for their job duties or required by law. Personal information learned through a complaint, investigation, accommodation request, injury report, domestic violence concern, or privacy concern must be kept confidential.

Reporting procedure

Workers must report workplace violence, harassment, respectful workplace concerns, privacy breaches, and domestic violence hazards that may affect the workplace as soon as reasonably practicable. Reports can be made verbally or in writing.

  1. If there is immediate danger, move to a safe place and contact emergency services. Notify a supervisor, manager, owner, or Human Resources as soon as it is safe to do so.
  2. Report the concern to a supervisor, manager, owner, Human Resources representative, or another management representative the worker is comfortable approaching.
  3. Provide the date, time, location, people involved, witnesses, description of what happened, documents or messages available, and any immediate safety concerns. Only share details that are necessary for safety or investigation.
  4. The person receiving the report must document the report, assess immediate safety needs, preserve confidentiality, and notify Human Resources or senior management as appropriate.
  5. If the complaint involves the worker’s direct supervisor, the worker may report to another manager, owner, or Human Resources representative.

Investigation and corrective action

The Company will investigate complaints and incidents in a fair, timely, and confidential manner appropriate to the nature and seriousness of the concern. Investigations may be conducted by Human Resources, management, the owner, or an external investigator where appropriate.

An investigation may include interviews, document review, electronic records review, worksite inspection, hazard assessment, consultation with the health and safety representative or committee where applicable, and interim controls to protect safety and privacy.

The Company will inform the parties involved of the results of the investigation and of corrective action to be taken to address the complaint or incident, while respecting privacy and confidentiality. Corrective action may include coaching, apology, training, work reassignment, workplace controls, privacy safeguards, discipline, removal from a work site, contract termination, or reporting to law enforcement or another authority where appropriate or required.

Confidentiality and no retaliation

Confidentiality will be maintained for all parties involved in a complaint, incident, domestic violence concern, privacy concern, investigation, or accommodation request, except where disclosure is necessary to investigate, take corrective action, inform parties of results or corrective action, inform workers of a specific or general threat of violence or potential violence, obtain professional or legal advice, administer employment or safety requirements, or comply with law.

Retaliation is prohibited. No worker will be disciplined, dismissed, threatened, intimidated, penalized, or treated adversely for making a good-faith report, participating in an investigation, requesting support, or raising a privacy or safety concern. Retaliation will be treated as a serious breach of this policy.

Training and communication

DS Home Computing Ltd will ensure workers receive training appropriate to their role. Training will be provided during new-hire orientation, when this policy is revised, and when required by a change in work, hazard assessment, incident, or legislation.

Training will include:

  • Recognition of violence, domestic violence hazards, harassment, bullying, discrimination, retaliation, and privacy breaches.
  • How to respond to violence and harassment, including how to get assistance.
  • How to report, investigate, and document complaints and incidents.
  • Confidentiality expectations and limits.
  • Privacy duties related to employee records, investigation records, electronic systems, client information, and Company records.
  • The connection between this policy, the HSMS, hazard assessments, emergency response, fit for work, inspections, and corrective action.

Records

The Company will maintain records related to reports, investigations, hazard assessments, training, corrective action, accommodations, and privacy matters in a secure manner. Records will be retained according to Company record retention requirements and applicable law. Access will be limited to authorized personnel with a legitimate need to know.

Review

This policy and related prevention measures will be reviewed at the earliest of the following:

  • when an incident of violence, harassment, domestic violence affecting the workplace, or a significant privacy concern indicates a review is required;
  • when a change to work or a work site could affect the potential for violence, harassment, or privacy risk;
  • when the joint health and safety committee, health and safety representative, affected workers, Human Resources, or management requests a review; or
  • at least every three years.

The Company will revise this policy or the related prevention plan as necessary following a review. Affected workers, the health and safety representative, or the joint health and safety committee will be consulted as far as reasonably practicable where required.

Domestic Violence, Respectful Workplace, Privacy and Progressive Discipline

Corrective action may be given at any level depending on the severity of the violation, the risk created, prior conduct, whether the conduct was intentional, and the need to protect workers, clients, visitors, the public, Company records, and Company property. This policy supports the HSMS Enforcement and Corrective Action Policy, including verbal warning, written warning, suspension, and termination where appropriate.

Instances where an employee may receive coaching, retraining, a verbal warning, or a written warning include, but are not limited to:

  • minor disrespectful communication that is corrected immediately and does not create a serious safety, privacy, or human rights risk;
  • failure to follow reporting, documentation, privacy, or confidentiality procedures where no serious harm resulted; or
  • unintentional misuse of Company records that is promptly reported and corrected.

Instances where an employee may receive a written warning, suspension, removal from a work site, or other serious corrective action include, but are not limited to:

  • repeated disrespectful conduct, bullying, harassment, or failure to follow a corrective action plan;
  • failure by a supervisor or manager to respond to a reported violence, harassment, domestic violence, or privacy concern;
  • unauthorized access to, use of, or disclosure of personal information;
  • retaliation or attempted retaliation against a person who reports or participates in a process under this policy.

Instances where an employee or subcontractor may be terminated with cause, removed from a site, or have their contract terminated immediately include, but are not limited to:

  • actual or attempted physical violence, serious threats, stalking, coercion, or intimidation connected to the workplace;
  • sexual violence, sexual harassment, or serious discriminatory harassment;
  • knowingly making a malicious complaint or deliberately interfering with an investigation;
  • serious or intentional privacy breach, including unauthorized disclosure of investigation records, medical information, employee personal information, client information, or domestic violence safety information;
  • refusal to comply with reasonable controls put in place to protect workers from violence, harassment, or privacy risk.
