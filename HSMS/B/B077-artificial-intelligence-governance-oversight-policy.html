ESG-GOV-POL-008Artificial Intelligence Governance & Oversight Policy

Introduction

Artificial Intelligence technologies present both strategic opportunities and material enterprise risks. The integration of AI into operational, analytical, reporting, and administrative processes may affect corporate governance, regulatory compliance, data protection, human rights, workforce management, and reputational integrity.

As AI adoption expands, enterprise-level oversight is required to ensure its deployment aligns with corporate values, ESG commitments, and applicable legal obligations in both Canada and the United States.

Purpose

This Policy establishes the governance framework for Artificial Intelligence oversight within DS Home Computing Ltd. It defines accountability, ethical principles, and risk management expectations for AI systems deployed across the organization.

This Policy ensures that AI use:

Aligns with corporate governance standards

Protects stakeholder interests

Preserves human oversight

Mitigates regulatory and reputational risk

Supports ESG commitments

Roles & Responsibilities

Executive Leadership

Maintains ultimate accountability for enterprise AI risk exposure.

Approves high-risk AI implementations.

Reviews annual AI governance summary.

ESG Coordinator

Oversees AI governance framework.

Monitors AI-related risk exposure.

Ensures alignment with ESG commitments.

Coordinates annual AI review.

Information Technology/Administration

Evaluates cybersecurity risks of AI systems.

Confirms data protection compliance.

Ensures access controls.

Department Managers

Act as AI System Owners within their functions.

Ensure operational compliance with AI policies.

Maintain human oversight controls.

Human Resources

Monitors AI systems affecting workforce decisions.

Ensures non-discriminatory practices.

Employees and Contractors

Comply with all AI governance controls.

Report AI-related risks or misuse.

Scope

This Policy applies to all Artificial Intelligence systems used across the organization, including:

Internally deployed AI tools

AI-enabled enterprise software

Third-party AI vendors

Generative AI applications

Predictive analytics platforms

This Policy applies to all corporate functions and jurisdictions of operation.

Terms and Definitions

Artificial Intelligence (AI): Software systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human cognitive functions, including prediction, content generation, classification, or decision-support.

Enterprise AI System: Any AI tool integrated into operational, reporting, governance, or workforce processes.

High-Risk AI: AI systems that may materially affect safety, employment, financial reporting, regulatory compliance, ESG disclosures, or contractual obligations.

AI Governance Framework: The structured system of policies, procedures, oversight mechanisms, and documentation used to manage AI-related risks.

Procedure

Human Oversight

AI systems shall not replace human judgment in decisions affecting safety, employment, regulatory reporting, financial disclosure, or contractual commitments.

Ethical Use

AI shall be deployed in a manner consistent with corporate human rights, anti-discrimination, and ethical conduct commitments.

Risk-Based Implementation

All AI systems shall undergo formal risk assessment prior to deployment. Higher-risk systems require executive approval.

Transparency & Documentation

AI deployments shall be documented within the AI Usage Register. Material AI applications shall be auditable and reviewable.

Continuous Monitoring

AI systems shall be periodically reviewed for performance, compliance, and emerging risk exposure.

2025-2026 Regulatory Update - AI Governance Landscape

Canada proposed Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA), Part 3 of Bill C-27, died when Parliament was prorogued on January 6, 2025. AIDA is no longer pending legislation and references to it as a forthcoming compliance obligation are removed from this policy. Federal AI governance in Canada is now guided by the voluntary Directive on Automated Decision-Making (Treasury Board Secretariat) for federal institutions, and by the Canadian Artificial Intelligence Safety Institute (CAISI), launched November 2024 as part of a CAD $2.4 billion AI safety initiative.

Ontario Employer AI Disclosure Requirement (2024): Ontario is the first Canadian province to require employers to disclose the use of AI in employment-related decisions. If DS Home Computing uses AI tools including AI-assisted screening, scheduling, or performance monitoring that affect Ontario employees, disclosure to those employees is legally required. DS Home Computing must audit current AI tool usage and implement disclosure notices where required.

US Federal AI Policy Change (January 2025): US Executive Order 14110 on AI (October 2023) was rescinded January 20, 2025. The current US federal AI posture is deregulatory. Sector-specific AI requirements (such as OSHA guidance on AI in safety-critical operations) remain applicable. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) remains the voluntary best-practice standard for US AI governance and DS Home Computing continues to apply it.

Applicable Governance Framework Post-AIDA: DS Home Computing AI governance is guided by: (1) CAISI guidance as it develops (Canada); (2) Ontario employer AI disclosure obligations where applicable; (3) NIST AI RMF 1.0 (voluntary US standard); (4) EU AI Act (in force August 2024) if DS Home Computing AI systems are offered to EU clients; (5) PIPEDA/CPPA privacy obligations for AI systems processing personal information. Human judgment must be retained for all decisions affecting safety, employment, financial disclosure, and regulatory reporting.
