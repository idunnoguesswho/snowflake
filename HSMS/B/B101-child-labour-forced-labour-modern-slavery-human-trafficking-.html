ESG-SOC-POL-002 Child Labour, Forced Labour, Modern Slavery & Human Trafficking Policy

Introduction

DS Home Computing Ltd is committed to conducting business in a manner that respects human dignity and fundamental human rights. Modern slavery, child labour, forced labour, and human trafficking are serious violations of human rights and are not tolerated in any form within DS Home Computing Ltd operations or its supply chain.

This policy establishes DS Home Computing Ltd’s expectations and commitments to prevent, identify, and address risks related to modern slavery, child labour, forced labour, and human trafficking across all jurisdictions in which the organization operates.

Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to:

Affirm DS Home Computing Ltd’s zero-tolerance stance on modern slavery, child labour, forced labour, and human trafficking

Ensure compliance with applicable international, national, and local labour laws

Establish clear expectations for employees, contractors, and suppliers

Support ethical, responsible, and sustainable business practices

Roles & Responsibilities

Management

Demonstrate leadership and accountability for compliance with this policy

Ensure appropriate resources are available to support prevention and oversight

Take action where violations are identified

Human Resources/Safety/ESG Coordinator

Coordinate training and awareness initiatives

Support due diligence, monitoring, and investigations

Track and report trends and corrective actions

Supervisors

Promote awareness of labour rights and ethical conduct

Monitor work environments for potential risks or indicators of non-compliance

Escalate concerns promptly

Workers

Comply with this policy and applicable labour laws

Report suspected or actual violations without fear of retaliation

Scope

This policy applies to all DS Home Computing Ltd employees, contractors, subcontractors, temporary workers, and business partners across all operations, projects, and countries where DS Home Computing Ltd conducts business..

Terms and Definitions

Child LabourWork that deprives children of their childhood, potential, or dignity, or that is harmful to physical or mental development, as defined by ILO Conventions No. 138 and No. 182.

Forced LabourAll work or service extracted from a person under threat, coercion, or penalty and for which the person has not offered themselves voluntarily, as defined by ILO Convention No. 29.

Modern SlaverySituations of exploitation where individuals cannot refuse or leave work due to threats, violence, coercion, deception, or abuse of power.

Human TraffickingThe recruitment, transportation, transfer, harboring, or receipt of persons by means of threat, force, coercion, or deception for the purpose of exploitation, as defined by the Palermo Protocol.

Policy

DS Home Computing Ltd maintains zero tolerance for modern slavery, child labour, forced labour, and human trafficking.

Prohibition of Child Labour

DS Home Computing Ltd will not employ individuals below the minimum legal working age or below 16 years of age, whichever is higher

Individuals under 18 years of age will not be assigned hazardous work or work that interferes with education or well-being

Prohibition of Forced Labour and Human Trafficking

No individual shall be required to work against their will

Coercion, threats, abuse, involuntary servitude, or withholding of personal documents is prohibited

Human trafficking in any form is strictly prohibited

Working Hours and Conditions

Working hours and conditions must comply with applicable labour laws

No individual under 18 years of age shall be required to work late-night or early-morning hours contrary to legal requirements

Supply Chain Expectations

Suppliers and contractors are expected to uphold the same standards

Where violations are identified, DS Home Computing Ltd may require corrective action or terminate the business relationship

Reporting and Non-Retaliation

Individuals are encouraged to report concerns without fear of retaliation

Reports will be investigated promptly and confidentially where possible

Training

DS Home Computing Ltd will provide awareness and training related to modern slavery, child labour, forced labour, and human trafficking through:

Periodic safety or ethics meetings

Online or internal training programs

Awareness communications

Review

This policy shall be reviewed every three (3) years, or sooner if required due to legislative changes, operational changes, or emerging risks.

Canada S-211 Fighting Against Forced Labour and Child Labour in Supply Chains Act

Canada Fighting Against Forced Labour and Child Labour in Supply Chains Act (Bill S-211) came into force January 1, 2024. Updated guidance was issued by Public Safety Canada in November 2024. DS Home Computing Ltd must annually assess whether it meets the reporting threshold and file annual reports by May 31 each year with the Minister of Public Safety.

Reporting Threshold: Entities incorporated, formed, or operating in Canada, or listed on a Canadian stock exchange, that meet at least two of: (a) CAD $20 million in assets; (b) CAD $40 million in annual revenue; (c) 250 or more employees must file annually. DS Home Computing must assess and retain documentation of its threshold status each year.

Annual Report Requirements (due May 31): Due May 31 each year. Report must describe steps taken in the prior year to prevent and reduce the risk of forced and child labour, covering: (a) structure, activities, and supply chains; (b) due diligence policies and processes; (c) risk assessment and management; (d) remediation measures; (e) training; (f) effectiveness assessment. Report must be approved by senior management, published on the DS Home Computing website, and submitted electronically to Public Safety Canada. False or misleading statements may result in fines.

Supplier Due Diligence: Tier 1 suppliers must complete a forced and child labour due diligence questionnaire annually. Supplier contracts must include representations and warranties regarding compliance with applicable forced labour and child labour laws. Where violations are identified, DS Home Computing may require immediate corrective action plans with defined timelines or terminate the supplier relationship.
