Respiratory Protection Code of Practice- Canada

HSE-COP-001Respiratory Protection Code of Practice- Canada

Introduction

DS Home Computing is committed to protecting workers from health hazards associated with airborne contaminants and oxygen-deficient atmospheres. Respiratory hazards may be present during commissioning, maintenance, testing, confined space entry, emergency response, and activities involving hazardous substances.

This Code of Practice establishes the minimum requirements for the selection, use, inspection, maintenance, fit testing, and training related to respiratory protective equipment (RPE) for all DS Home Computing operations in Canada. Respiratory protection is required when engineering and administrative controls are not practicable, insufficient, or during emergency situations.

Purpose

The purpose of this Code of Practice is to ensure that:

Workers are adequately protected from inhalation hazards and oxygen-deficient atmospheres

Respiratory protective equipment is selected and used appropriately based on hazard assessments

Respiratory protection programs comply with applicable Canadian occupational health and safety legislation and CSA standards

Workers receive appropriate training and fit testing before using respiratory protection

Roles & Responsibilities

Management

Implementing and maintaining this Code of Practice across all Canadian operations

Ensuring appropriate respiratory protective equipment is available

Ensuring hazard assessments identify respiratory hazards

Ensuring workers are fit-tested and trained as required

Ensuring inspection, maintenance, and testing requirements are met

Maintaining required records

Supervisors

Verifying respiratory hazards are identified prior to work

Ensuring appropriate respiratory protection is available and used correctly

Ensuring workers are fit-tested and authorized to use assigned respirators

Monitoring proper use, storage, and condition of equipment

Removing defective or non-compliant equipment from service

Submitting fit test and inspection records as required

Workers

Wearing respiratory protection when required

Using respiratory equipment in accordance with training

Inspecting equipment prior to each use

Maintaining and storing assigned equipment properly

Participating in required fit testing and training

Reporting defects or concerns immediately

Scope

This Code of Practice applies to all DS Home Computing workers, contractors, and visitors in Canada who may be exposed to:

Airborne contaminants exceeding occupational exposure limits (OELs)

Oxygen-deficient atmospheres (<19.5% oxygen)

Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health (IDLH) atmospheres

It applies to all DS Home Computing field, shop, office, and emergency response activities within Canada.

Terms and Definitions

Air-Purifying Respirator (APR): A respirator that removes contaminants from ambient air using filters or cartridges. APRs shall not be used in oxygen-deficient or IDLH atmospheres.

Assigned Protection Factor (APF): The level of respiratory protection provided by a properly functioning respirator.

IDLH (Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health): An atmosphere that poses an immediate or delayed threat to life or health.

OEL (Occupational Exposure Limit): The maximum allowable airborne concentration of a substance.

SCBA (Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus): A respirator that supplies breathable air from a compressed air cylinder carried by the user.

SABA / Supplied-Air Breathing Apparatus: A respirator that supplies air from a remote source via an airline, with emergency egress air where required.

Fit Testing: A qualitative or quantitative method used to verify a respirator’s seal to the wearer’s face.

Procedures

Hazard Assessment

A hazard assessment must be completed before work begins where respiratory hazards may exist

The assessment shall identify:

Airborne contaminants and potential concentrations

Oxygen levels

Potential for IDLH conditions

Respiratory protection is required when engineering or administrative controls are not practicable or during emergencies

Selection of Respiratory Protection

Respiratory protection shall be selected in accordance with CSA Z94.4 – Selection, Use, and Care of Respirators.

SCBA / Supplied-Air Respirators

SCBA or supplied-air respirators shall be used when:

Oxygen concentration is less than 19.5%

The atmosphere is IDLH or unknown

Contaminant concentrations exceed APR capabilities

Full-face, pressure-demand SCBA or supplied-air respirators with emergency egress air shall be used

Egress air is for evacuation only and shall not be used to extend work time

Air-Purifying Respirators

APRs may only be used when:

Oxygen concentration is ≥19.5%

Contaminants and concentrations are known

Proper cartridges or filters are selected

APRs shall not be used in IDLH or oxygen-deficient atmospheres

Cartridges and filters shall be changed:

In accordance with manufacturer instructions

At the start of each shift where end-of-service indicators are not present

Fit Testing

Fit testing is required:

Prior to initial use

At least annually

When a different respirator is used

When physical changes may affect fit

Qualitative and quantitative fit testing methods are permitted

Facial hair that interferes with the face seal is prohibited for tight-fitting respirators

Seal checks must be performed before each use

Use of Respiratory Protection

Respirators shall be used only for their intended purpose

Modifications to respirators are prohibited

Proper donning, doffing, and use procedures must be followed

Maintenance, Cleaning, and Storage

Respirators shall be cleaned and disinfected after each use

Cleaning shall follow manufacturer instructions

Respirators shall be stored:

In a clean, dry location

Protected from damage, sunlight, and contaminants

Inspection and Testing

Before each use: Worker inspection required

Monthly: Emergency-use respirators

Annually: SCBA and supplied-air systems by a qualified technician

Compressed breathing air shall comply with CSA Z180.1

Cylinders shall be hydrostatically tested in accordance with Transport Canada requirements

Training

Workers required to use respiratory protection shall receive training on:

Respiratory hazards and health effects

Respirator selection, limitations, and capabilities

Proper use, inspection, and storage

Fit testing procedures

Emergency and equipment failure procedures

Training shall be provided:

Prior to initial use

When equipment or hazards change

At intervals required by legislation or company policy
