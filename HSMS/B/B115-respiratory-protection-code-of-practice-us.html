HSE-COP-002Respiratory Protection Code of Practice- US

Introduction

DS Home Computing recognizes that exposure to airborne contaminants and oxygen-deficient atmospheres presents a significant health risk to workers. Respiratory hazards may arise during commissioning, construction, maintenance, testing, troubleshooting, confined space entry, emergency response, and work involving dusts, fumes, mists, gases, vapours, sprays, or oxygen displacement.

This Code of Practice establishes the requirements for a comprehensive Respiratory Protection Program in accordance with U.S. Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) regulations. The program addresses hazard evaluation, respirator selection, medical evaluation, fit testing, training, use, inspection, maintenance, recordkeeping, and program review.

Respiratory protection shall be used only when engineering and administrative controls are not feasible, are insufficient, or during emergency conditions.

Purpose

The purpose of this Code of Practice is to:

Protect workers from inhalation hazards and oxygen-deficient atmospheres

Prevent occupational illness, injury, and long-term health effects

Establish uniform respiratory protection requirements across U.S. operations

Ensure compliance with applicable OSHA regulations and recognized standards

Define roles, responsibilities, procedures, and documentation requirements

Ensure respirators are selected, used, and maintained correctly

Roles & Responsibilities

Management

Establishing, implementing, and maintaining a written Respiratory Protection Program

Ensuring respiratory hazards are identified and evaluated

Ensuring engineering and administrative controls are implemented where feasible

Providing appropriate NIOSH-approved respirators at no cost to employees

Appointing a qualified Respiratory Protection Coordinator

Ensuring employees receive required medical evaluations, fit testing, and training

Ensuring respirators are properly stored, maintained, inspected, repaired, and replaced

Ensuring program records are maintained and protected

Supporting enforcement and corrective actions

Reviewing program effectiveness

Supervisors

Verifying respiratory hazards are identified prior to work

Ensuring workers use respiratory protection when required

Confirming workers are medically cleared, fit-tested, and trained

Ensuring correct respirator type, cartridge, or filter selection

Enforcing compliance with facial hair, seal, and use requirements

Ensuring respirators are inspected prior to use

Removing defective respirators from service

Stopping work when respiratory protection requirements are not met

Reporting respirator-related incidents or failures

Workers

Participating in medical evaluations, fit testing, and training

Using respirators in accordance with training and manufacturer instructions

Inspecting respirators before each use

Performing user seal checks each time a respirator is donned

Maintaining facial hair so it does not interfere with the seal

Cleaning, maintaining, and storing respirators properly

Leaving the work area immediately if respirator effectiveness is compromised

Reporting defects, malfunctions, or concerns immediately

Not modifying or misusing respiratory equipment

Health and Safety Team

Administer the Respiratory Protection Program

Maintain medical, fit-testing, and training records

Assist with hazard assessments and respirator selection

Ensure availability of appropriate respirators

Coordinate fit testing and training

Evaluate program effectiveness and recommend improvements

Scope

This Code of Practice applies to all DS Home Computing employees and U.S. job sites where:

Respiratory protection is required, or

Respiratory protection is used voluntarily, except for voluntary use of filtering facepiece respirators where no respiratory hazard exists

This applies to all respirator types, including APRs, PAPRs, SARs, SCBA, and escape-only respirators.

Terms and Definitions

Air-Purifying Respirator (APR)

A respirator that removes specific air contaminants by passing ambient air through filters, cartridges, or canisters before inhalation. APRs do not supply oxygen and shall not be used in oxygen-deficient or IDLH atmospheres.

Assigned Protection Factor (APF)

The level of respiratory protection that a properly functioning respirator is expected to provide to a user when the respirator is correctly selected, fitted, used, and maintained. APFs are established by OSHA and are used to determine the suitability of a respirator for a specific exposure.

Atmosphere-Supplying Respirator (SAR)

A respirator that supplies breathable air to the user from a source independent of the ambient atmosphere, including supplied-air respirators and self-contained breathing apparatus.

Breakthrough

The point at which a contaminant passes through a respirator’s filter, cartridge, or canister and is detected by the user, indicating loss of effective protection.

End-of-Service-Life Indicator (ESLI)

A system that warns the respirator user that the filter, cartridge, or canister has reached the end of its useful service life.

Fit Test

A qualitative or quantitative evaluation conducted to verify that a specific make, model, style, and size of respirator properly seals to an individual’s face.

Fit Factor

A numerical value that represents the level of protection provided by a respirator during quantitative fit testing. The fit factor must meet or exceed OSHA minimum requirements for the respirator type.

Filtering Facepiece Respirator (FFR)

A negative-pressure particulate respirator with a filter as an integral part of the facepiece or with the entire facepiece composed of the filtering medium (commonly referred to as a “dust mask”).

Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health (IDLH)

An atmosphere that poses an immediate or delayed threat to life, would cause irreversible adverse health effects, or would impair an individual’s ability to escape from a hazardous environment.

Maximum Use Concentration (MUC)

The maximum airborne concentration of a contaminant from which a respirator can protect a user. The MUC is calculated by multiplying the APF of the respirator by the applicable exposure limit.

Medical Evaluation

An assessment conducted by a Physician or Licensed Health Care Professional (PLHCP) to determine an employee’s ability to safely wear a respirator without adverse health effects.

Negative-Pressure Respirator

A respirator in which air is drawn through the filter, cartridge, or canister by the user’s inhalation, creating negative pressure inside the facepiece.

Oxygen-Deficient Atmosphere

An atmosphere containing less than 19.5 percent oxygen by volume, which is considered hazardous and typically classified as IDLH.

Physician or Licensed Health Care Professional (PLHCP)

An individual whose legally permitted scope of practice allows them to independently provide or be delegated responsibility for health care services, including medical evaluations for respirator use.

Powered Air-Purifying Respirator (PAPR)

An air-purifying respirator that uses a blower to force ambient air through filters or cartridges to the facepiece, hood, or helmet.

Pressure-Demand Respirator

A respirator that maintains positive pressure inside the facepiece during inhalation and exhalation, reducing the likelihood of contaminant leakage.

Qualitative Fit Test (QLFT)

A pass/fail fit test that relies on the wearer’s subjective response to a test agent (e.g., saccharin, Bitrex, isoamyl acetate) to detect leakage into the respirator.

Quantitative Fit Test (QNFT)

A fit test that uses an instrument to measure the amount of leakage into the respirator and provides a numerical fit factor.

Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA)

An atmosphere-supplying respirator in which the user carries the breathing air supply, designed for use in IDLH or oxygen-deficient atmospheres.

Service Life

The period of time during which a respirator filter, cartridge, or canister provides adequate protection against contaminants.

Supplied-Air Respirator (SAR)

A respirator that supplies breathable air to the user from a remote source through an air hose, with or without an auxiliary escape air supply.

User Seal Check

An action conducted by the respirator wearer to verify that the respirator is properly seated and sealed to the face before entering a contaminated atmosphere.

Voluntary Respirator Use

The use of a respirator when it is not required by hazard assessment or regulation, but is worn at the employee’s discretion.

Procedures

Respiratory Hazard Identification and Exposure Assessment

Respiratory hazards shall be identified before work begins and whenever work conditions change.

Hazard identification shall consider:

Nature of airborne contaminants (dusts, fumes, mists, gases, vapours, sprays)

Physical and chemical properties of contaminants

Potential for oxygen displacement or consumption

Likelihood of IDLH conditions

Duration, frequency, and intensity of exposure

Activities requiring evaluation include, but are not limited to:

Hydrocarbon sampling and testing

Venting or bleeding of process systems

Instrumentation installation, calibration, and maintenance

Emergency response to uncontrolled releases

Confined space entry

Pumping, transfer, or disposal of fluids

Painting, welding, abrasive blasting, or chemical handling

Where monitoring data is unavailable, exposure shall be assumed to be worst-case.

Respiratory protection shall be required when:

Engineering controls are infeasible or insufficient

Administrative controls do not adequately reduce exposure

Emergency or non-routine work is performed

Selection of Respiratory Protective Equipment

Respirators shall be selected based on:

Identified hazards and concentrations

Oxygen concentration

Assigned Protection Factors (APFs)

Maximum Use Concentrations (MUCs)

Work conditions and duration

Only NIOSH-approved respirators shall be used.

Respirators shall be selected and approved by the Respiratory Protection Coordinator or designated competent person.

Selection shall ensure that respirators provide protection greater than the anticipated exposure, not equal to it.

IDLH and Unknown Atmospheres

Atmospheres shall be classified as IDLH when:

Contaminant concentrations are unknown

Oxygen concentration is <19.5%

There is potential for sudden release or atmospheric change

Only the following respirators are permitted:

Pressure-demand SCBA

Pressure-demand supplied-air respirator with auxiliary SCBA

Entry into IDLH atmospheres requires:

At least one trained attendant stationed outside the atmosphere

Continuous communication capability

Pre-planned rescue procedures

Emergency response capability appropriate to the hazard

Escape-only respirators shall be suitable for the specific hazard and shall not be used to continue work.

Oxygen-Deficient Atmospheres

Oxygen concentrations below 19.5% are prohibited without atmosphere-supplying respirators.

Oxygen-deficient atmospheres shall be treated as IDLH unless proven otherwise.

Air-purifying respirators are strictly prohibited.

Continuous oxygen monitoring shall be implemented where oxygen-deficient conditions may develop.

Use of Air-Purifying Respirators (APRs)

APRs may only be used when:

Oxygen levels are ≥19.5%

Contaminants and concentrations are known

Respirator APF exceeds the required MUC

Cartridge and filter selection shall be specific to the contaminant.

APRs shall not be used:

In IDLH atmospheres

Where warning properties are inadequate without ESLI

Cartridge change-out shall be controlled by:

End-of-Service-Life Indicators (ESLI), or

A written, data-driven change-out schedule

Change-out schedules shall account for:

Contaminant concentration

Breathing rate

Environmental conditions

Cartridge capacity

Cartridges shall be replaced immediately if:

Breakthrough is detected

Odour, taste, or irritation occurs

Resistance increases

Breathing Air Quality and Supplied-Air Systems

Supplied breathing air shall meet ANSI/CGA G-7.1 Grade D requirements.

Air supply systems shall be designed to prevent:

Contaminated intake air

Backflow

Moisture accumulation

Compressors shall be:

Maintained per manufacturer requirements

Equipped with CO monitoring and audible/visual alarms

Cylinders shall be:

Maintained and hydrostatically tested per DOT 49 CFR Parts 173 and 178

Clearly marked and traceable

Breathing air quality documentation shall be retained and available.

Medical Evaluation Procedures

Employees shall complete a medical evaluation prior to fit testing or respirator use.

Evaluations shall be conducted by a PLHCP.

Follow-up evaluations shall be conducted when:

Symptoms related to respirator use occur

Work conditions change

A supervisor or PLHCP identifies concern

Employees shall not be permitted to wear respirators without medical clearance.

Fit Testing Procedures

Fit testing shall be conducted:

Before initial respirator use

Annually

When respirator type, model, or size changes

After facial changes affecting fit

Fit testing shall follow OSHA-accepted protocols.

Fit test results shall be documented and retained.

Employees failing fit testing shall not be permitted to use the respirator until a proper fit is achieved.

Respirator Use Requirements

Respirators shall be donned, adjusted, and worn according to manufacturer instructions.

User seal checks shall be conducted every time the respirator is donned.

Facial hair or apparel interfering with the seal is prohibited.

Employees shall immediately leave the contaminated area if:

Respirator malfunction occurs

Breakthrough is detected

Physical distress occurs

Inspection, Maintenance, and Repair

Respirators shall be inspected:

Before each use

After cleaning

Monthly for emergency-use respirators

Inspections shall include:

Facepiece integrity

Valves and seals

Head straps and harness

Cylinders and regulators

Defective respirators shall be removed from service immediately.

Repairs shall only be performed by qualified personnel using manufacturer-approved parts.

Cleaning and Storage

Respirators shall be cleaned and disinfected after each use.

Cleaning shall follow OSHA Appendix B-2 or manufacturer guidance.

Respirators shall be stored:

In a clean, dry location

Protected from sunlight, chemicals, and deformation

Emergency-use respirators shall be stored for immediate access.

Training and Competency Verification

Employees shall receive training prior to respirator use.

Training shall be repeated:

Annually

When hazards or equipment change

Training shall include hands-on demonstrations.

Competency shall be verified and documented.

Program Evaluation and Continuous Improvement

The Respiratory Protection Program shall be evaluated periodically.

Evaluations shall consider:

User feedback

Incident reports

Fit test failure trends

Corrective actions shall be implemented and documented.

Emergency Response and Unplanned Releases

Respiratory protection requirements for emergency response shall be determined prior to response activities, where feasible.

Emergency response situations include, but are not limited to:

Uncontrolled releases of gases, vapours, or liquids

Oxygen-deficient atmospheres

Confined space emergencies

Fire, explosion, or chemical release incidents

Emergency responders shall:

Use pressure-demand SCBA or pressure-demand SAR with auxiliary SCBA

Be trained specifically in emergency respirator use

Be medically cleared and fit-tested for the equipment used

Escape-only respirators shall:

Be suitable for the specific hazard

Be clearly labelled for escape use only

Not be used to continue work or perform rescue

Emergency respiratory protection procedures shall be coordinated with:

Site Emergency Response Plans

Confined Space Programs

Hazard Communication Programs

Confined Space Entry Integration

Respiratory protection requirements for confined space entry shall be determined as part of the confined space hazard assessment.

Respiratory hazards shall be evaluated for:

Oxygen deficiency or enrichment

Toxic atmospheres

Potential for atmospheric changes during entry

Where respiratory hazards are present or may develop:

Atmosphere-supplying respirators shall be used

Continuous atmospheric monitoring shall be implemented

Respirator selection shall be compatible with:

Rescue procedures

Retrieval systems

Communications equipment

Respiratory protection requirements shall be documented on the confined space entry permit.

Multi-Employer and Contractor Worksites

Where DS Home Computing operates on multi-employer worksites:

Respiratory protection requirements shall be communicated to affected employers

Host employer respiratory requirements shall be reviewed

Contractors required to use respiratory protection shall:

Demonstrate compliance with OSHA respiratory protection requirements, or

Comply with DS Home Computing respiratory protection requirements

Contractor respirator use shall not be permitted unless:

Medical evaluation, fit testing, and training are verified

Respirator selection is appropriate for the hazard

Conflicting respiratory protection requirements shall be resolved prior to work.

Voluntary Respirator Use Procedures

Voluntary respirator use shall be permitted only when:

No respiratory hazard exists, and

Use does not create additional hazards

Employees voluntarily using respirators other than filtering facepieces shall:

Receive a medical evaluation

Be provided OSHA Appendix D information

Maintain respirators in a clean and sanitary condition

Voluntary use of filtering facepiece respirators does not require:

Medical evaluation

Fit testing

Inclusion in the written program

Voluntary respirator use shall be documented where applicable.

Respirator Issuance and Control

Respirators shall be issued and controlled to ensure:

Correct model, size, and configuration

Traceability to fit test records

Issued respirators shall not be shared unless:

Cleaned and disinfected prior to reuse

Re-inspected before use

Emergency-use respirators shall be:

Clearly identified

Stored for immediate access

Inspected and documented monthly

Recordkeeping and Documentation

The following records shall be maintained:

Medical clearance documentation

Fit test records

Training records

Inspection and maintenance records

Breathing air quality documentation

Medical records shall be retained for:

Duration of employment plus 30 years

Fit test and training records shall be retained for:

At least the duration of employment

Records shall be:

Legible

Secure

Available upon request to employees or regulators

Program Audits and Effectiveness Evaluation

The Respiratory Protection Program shall be evaluated periodically to ensure:

Proper respirator selection

Continued effectiveness

Employee compliance

Program evaluations shall consider:

Changes in workplace conditions

Fit test failure trends

Incident and near-miss reports

Employee feedback

Deficiencies identified during evaluations shall:

Be documented

Result in corrective action

Be tracked to completion

Non-Compliance and Corrective Action

Failure to comply with respiratory protection requirements may result in:

Removal from the work area

Retraining

Disciplinary action in accordance with company policy

Supervisors shall intervene immediately where improper respirator use is observed.

Repeated or serious violations shall be escalated to management.

Stop Work Authority

All employees have the authority to stop work if:

Respiratory protection requirements are not met

Atmospheric conditions become unsafe

Required equipment is unavailable or defective

Work shall not resume until:

Hazards are reassessed

Controls are implemented

Appropriate respiratory protection is in place

Interface With Other Safety Programs

This Respiratory Protection Code of Practice shall be implemented in coordination with:

Hazard Communication Program

Confined Space Program

Emergency Response Program

Personal Protective Equipment Program

Job Safety Analysis / Permit-to-Work Systems

Training

Training shall be provided before initial use, annually, and when conditions change. Training shall include hazards, respirator limitations, donning/doffing, inspection, maintenance, emergency response, and regulatory requirements.
