HSE-COP-005Confined Space Entry Code of Practice Canada

General

This Code of Practice provides guidance in recognizing, evaluating, and controlling potentially dangerous atmospheres in confined spaces that could result in injury. Work practices in confined spaces, pre-job hazard assessments and responsibilities of key personnel are discussed. Practices for cleaning, isolation, atmospheric testing, ventilation, temporary heating, and using electrically operated equipment are also discussed.

This Code of Practice applies to all field sites where confined space entry work is performed. The type, size, and location of operations vary, so it is necessary to use a site-specific work plan for confined space entry.

Characteristics of a Confined Space

A confined space is a space that:

I.is large enough and configured so that a worker can physically enter and perform assigned work

II.has limited or restricted means for entry or exit

III.is not designed for continuous human occupancy.

Note: If the space in question does not meet all three of the above criteria, it is not a confined space and these practices do not apply.

Confined space entry practices must be followed when the confined space has any of the following characteristics:

1.It contains or has a potential to contain a hazardous atmosphere.

2.It contains a material that has the potential for engulfing an entrant.

3.It has an internal configuration such that an entrant could be trapped or asphyxiated by inwardly converging walls or by a floor that slopes downward and tapers to a smaller cross- section.

4.It contains any other recognized serious safety or health hazard.

Confined spaces are normally considered to include, but are not limited to, the following:

Areas where access or egress is limited by piping, valves, or substructures

Atmospheric storage tanks, ballast fuel

Excavations in excess of 1.5 meters deep

Treaters

Mole sieve and desiccant towers

Vessels, drums, towers

Boilers, reboilers, or exchangers

Tank cars or tank trucks

Aerial coolers

Training

Anyone that enters a confined space is required to have current Confined Space Entry certification. It will be ensured that a worker assigned duties related to confined space entry is trained by a competent person in recognizing hazards associated with working in confined spaces and performing their duties in a safe and healthy manner. Each affected employee will be trained prior to initial assignment, prior to a change in assigned duties, or if a new hazard has been created or special deviations have occurred.

Documented records of employee training will be kept.

Confined Entry Permit

No personnel will entry a confined space without a valid entry permit and attendant. DS HOME COMPUTING will establish an entry permit system for confined space that will:

List the names of each worker that enters the confined space and the reason for their entry,

Gives the location of the confined space,

Specifies the time during which an entry permit is valid,

Takes into account the work being done in the confined space,

Takes into account the code of practice requirements for entering, being in and leaving a confined space or the cancellation of a permit.

The employer will ensure that, before a worker enters a confined space, an entry permit is properly completed and signed by a competent worker and a copy kept readily available.

All records of entry and work in a confined space, including permits and testing will be kept for not less than one year if no incident or unplanned event occurred during the entry or two years if an incident or unplanned event occurred during the entry.

At no time will a single attendant monitor more than one confined space.

Responsibilities

Entry Supervisor

1.The entry supervisor must know the hazards that may be encountered during the entry and be able to recognize the behavioral effects that exposure to a hazardous condition may have on entrants.

2.The entry supervisor must verify that appropriate steps have been taken to make the space safe for entry and record this before allowing entry.

3.The entry supervisor must hold a meeting with entrants and attendants before entry. At the meeting, the entry supervisor must review the hazards associated with the space to be entered, the control measures in place to mitigate the hazards, the results of air monitoring within the space, and prohibited conditions that would require an evacuation of the space. He/she must also verify that the entrants and attendants are trained and understand their duties associated with the entry and work in the confined space.

4.The entry supervisor must terminate the entry and cancel the permit when

(1)the work is completed,

(2)a prohibited condition arises in the space, or

(3)an evacuation of the space is ordered. If the permit is terminated, the space must be re-evaluated before issuing a new permit to complete the work.

5.The entry supervisor must remove unauthorized individuals who attempt to enter the permitted space.

6.The entry supervisor must verify the capability and availability of rescue services and the means for summoning them prior to authorizing entry into a confined space.

7.When responsibility for a permit space entry is transferred to a new entry supervisor, the entry supervisor accepting the responsibility for the space must verify that the operations within the space are consistent with the permit.

Entrants

1.Entrants must know the hazards that may be encountered during the entry and be able to recognize when those hazards are present.

2.Entrants must properly use the equipment provided for entry into the confined space.

3.Entrants must maintain communication with the attendant.

4.Entrants must tell the attendant whenever:

(a)the entrant recognizes any warning sign, or symptom of exposure to a dangerous situation, or

(b)a prohibited condition is detected.

5.Entrants must immediately exit the permit space whenever

(a)an order to evacuate is given,

(b)a warning sign or symptom of exposure is recognized,

(c)a prohibited condition is detected, or (d) an evacuation alarm is sounded.

Attendant

An attendant is a worker who is trained in the hazards of confined spaces and whose primary responsibility is to monitor and assist the workers in the confined space.

Assistance includes maintaining communication with the workers via an adequate communication system, calling for emergency rescue, providing confined space workers with fresh air packs and other personal protective equipment.

The attendant is not to enter the confined space, and his/her location and activities will be determined by the hazard assessment and the resulting confined space plan.

The attendant has to be located outside and near the entrance of the confined space. As well he/she must be in constant communication with the workers inside the space and able to immediately summon a rescue response should it be required.

The attendant must be able to send and receive information to and from the workers inside the confined space in order to perform the duties of the attendant. How that is achieved is not specified; therefore, communications may be verbal or may include hand signals, radios, etc. The method will be determined by the employer and set out in the plan, based on the types of hazards identified in the assessment and the physical aspects of the confined space.

Safe Operating Practices

Where applicable, personnel must proceed with the following safe operating practices:

Pre-plan all jobs - This ensures that workers perform the job safely and in an environmentally responsible manner.

Carefully select workers - If more than one worker will be performing all or part of the confined space entry work, they must be qualified (medically approved to wear a respirator, fit tested as applicable, trained, and provided with PPE). If more than one worker performs the entry, proper co-ordination and communication must be in place to ensure that one worker is not exposing the other worker(s) to unplanned hazards. It must be ensured that personnel not authorized by the employer do not enter the confined space. There will be limited traffic in the area of the confined space, in order to prevent hazards for those that have entered the confined space.

Pre-job Hazard Assessment

The work location must be inspected before beginning work and the hazard assessment documented on the pre-job hazard assessment along with control strategies.

Conduct a planning meeting with key personnel involved in the intended work, and identify the following:

What work is required to be done?

What is the best method of isolation/lockouts?

What are the hazards involved during all phases of the work?

What equipment is required?

Is there a workable emergency plan?

Every job must be reviewed to determine whether or not it is a confined space and if there are adequate safeguards in place to allow safe entry, or if additional precautions need to be taken.

The following information will be covered:

Safety and protective equipment required

Special procedures/work limitations, including time limitations

Requirements for a safety watch (including qualifications)

Gas and oxygen tests required before entry

Conditions requiring re-testing and tests needed

Blanking and blinding of all lines leading to the confined space

Tools and utilities permitted in the confined space

Methods for elimination of hazards

Emergency response procedures and equipment

The requirements for safe entry will, in part, be determined by the type of space to be entered and the type of work to be performed.

Identify the Hazards – For each confined space entry, a hazard assessment must be done to determine what hazards may exist. Entry into confined spaces can expose workers to hazards that include: Toxic, oxygen-deficient, or flammable atmospheres, either as a normal characteristic of the space or as the product of work processes conducted while in the space;

Explosion

Electrical hazards, (including static)

Operation of equipment normally in the space or brought into it (i.e., moving parts)

Temperatures outside of comfort limits (i.e., excessive heat or cold)

Noise

Dust

Develop a Site-Specific Work Plan – Based on the results of the pre-job hazard assessment, a site-specific work plan can be developed such that the hazard can be eliminated or safety precautions taken to protect the worker from the hazard. It is generally preferable to eliminate the hazard by isolation and cleansing using chemicals, steam or water flushing, etc. If a confined space can be totally isolated and freed of hazardous material and the atmosphere verified to be hazard free by a competent worker, then the work can proceed without the need of additional precaution.

Work in the confined space may result in hazards being introduced to the confined space. In some vessels, mechanical hazards are a problem that must be considered. If such work will result in hazards, additional precautions must be undertaken to eliminate those hazards. The number of workers in the space must be restricted to the minimum required to do the job safely. No worker will enter or remain in a confined space unless it is ensured that an effective rescue can be carried out.

Isolation – When isolating, personnel must proceed with the following work practices:

Refer to: Equipment Isolation safe work procedures

Electrical equipment, including cathodic equipment, must be locked out and tagged in accordance with Lockout/Tagout safe work procedures

Discharge and/or isolate all other sources of energy (i.e., mechanical, hydraulic, gravitational) that could affect equipment in the area of the workers.

Do not take any high-pressure cylinders (propane, acetylene, carbon dioxide) into the confined space, except for respiratory protective equipment.

Do not leave acetylene equipment and hoses unattended in a confined space.

Cleaning and Ventilation – When cleaning and ventilating, personnel must proceed with the following safe operating practices:

Vessels must be drained and depressurized, then cleaned by flushing, steaming, or chemical neutralization. All clean-out doors and manway doors must be opened to facilitate ventilation. Additional ventilation may be required, depending on the results of the gas tests.

Use natural or mechanical ventilation to ventilate confined spaces, especially when the possibility exists, during the work, of generating toxic, explosive or flammable vapors (as when applying tank linings, cleaning a tank, or welding in vessels).

Proper bonding is required to prevent any possible issues with static electricity.

When cleaning confined spaces, consider the nature of the product and residue and use the best and most practical method known. Take special precautions with sludge, hydrocarbons or pyrophoric deposits. They may cling to surfaces or hang up in such vessel internals as spargers or dip pipes.

If using steam or inert gases, take extra precautions to cool the confined space and ensure adequate oxygen supply.

To prevent spontaneous combustion of some process material sludges, take special precautions such as continuous water flooding and immediate immersion in disposal containers.

Electrical Equipment – While work is being performed in a vessel, all electrical equipment used in the vessel must be protected by ground fault circuit interrupters. Personnel must proceed with the following steps:

Immediately before taking any electrical equipment into a confined space, visually check and test it for ground continuity.

In confined spaces where there is a possibility of flammable vapors, gases, or dust; ensure that all electrical equipment, lights and plug-in receptacles used are explosion proof and that all attached plugs and receptacles are located outside the confined space.

Be cautious of generating static electricity with the handling of high-pressure steam or air hoses, inert gases or sandblasting equipment.

Temporary Heating – to heat a confined space for workers, locate the heaters outside the space and run the hot air into the space through ducts. Do not use direct-fired heaters that discharge exhaust gases with the heated air. To prevent redirecting any harmful or noxious exhaust gases into the confined space, vent heaters in accordance with the manufacturer’s instruction. When heating spaces that may contain toxic, explosive, or flammable materials; reassess the amount of ventilation required.

It will be ensured that there is a safe and effective means of entry and exit to a confined space. Depending on the confined space, there will be different means of entry that may include secure steps, temporary platforms and handrails. Whatever the method used, it must be kept clear of materials and traffic to ensure safe entry and exit for personnel inside the confined space and emergency response members.

Communication – Personnel must proceed with the following safe communication practices:

In situations where normal voice contact is not possible, establish an alternative system of communication between the safety watch and the persons(s) in the confined space before entry and maintain it at all times.

A system of communication between the safety watch and support help must also be established and checked ahead of time.

Safety watch will consist of a competent worker, who is trained in evacuation procedures and present outside a confined space. The safety-watch person will keep track at all times the number of workers in the confined space. The safety-watch person will not leave the area until all persons inside the confined space have left, or another tending worker is in place.

An emergency response plan will include the emergency procedures to be followed if there is an accident or other emergency, including the procedures to evacuate the confined space immediately when an alarm is activated, if the concentration of oxygen inside the confined space drops below 19.5 percent by volume or exceeds 23.0 percent by volume or if there is a significant change in the amount of hazardous substances inside the confined space.

For detailed information, refer to the Hydrogen Sulfide Code of Practice and the Respiratory

Protection Code of Practice.

Atmospheric Testing and Monitoring

Atmospheric testing must be conducted before initial entry and after job interruptions into the confined space. The testing will be performed by a competent worker, to verify the oxygen level is between 19.5% and 23.0% by volume. This testing will also be done to identify the amount of toxic, flammable or explosive substances that may be present. Personnel must compare actual readings to the “acceptable atmosphere” values to determine if the atmosphere is safe for entry. Continuous monitoring may be needed to ensure a safe work environment. All workers must agree on a safe way to perform the job before beginning work. The results of the testing must be recorded and kept for referencing.

Monitoring of the atmosphere must be done to ensure the confined space does not become contaminated.

When testing atmosphere in a confined space, personnel must follow the steps listed below:

Ensure that explosive readings are less than 10% of the Lower Explosive Limit (LEL).

Note: Atmosphere must contain at least 16% oxygen for gas detection instruments to properly measure combustible vapor (LEL) and carbon monoxide (CO) levels.

Ensure only trained personnel perform the atmospheric test. Personnel must be retrained at least every 3 years. Wear breathing equipment during testing.

Test for excess or insufficient oxygen before any confined space entry. Monitor the oxygen level continuously where the atmosphere cannot be guaranteed. When the concentration of oxygen is less than 19.5%, full-faced self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA) or supplied air breathing apparatus (SABA) must be used by every worker entering the confined space.

Test of explosive or flammable vapor levels before entering any confined space. If the level is above 10% of the LEL, work must not proceed until the LEL level is reduced to below 10%. Monitor LEL continuously where the atmosphere cannot be guaranteed. Where lined vessels are being entered, consider the possibility of entrapped flammables in bulged or blistered linings. Note: if explosive readings are less than 10% of the LEL, the oxygen test must be greater than 16% before LEL and carbon monoxide (CO) tests are valid.

Where H2S may be present, follow the H2S Code of Practice.

Where other toxic vapors, fumes, dusts, and mists may exist; perform appropriate tests.

Have a certified technician remove or shield any radioactive sources.

Wear personal protective equipment and/or provide adequate ventilation where toxic gases above Occupational Exposure Limits (OEL’s) are present and cannot be reasonably eliminated.

Record measures and procedures taken of the pre-job hazard assessment.

Personal protective equipment (PPE)

Wear the proper personal protective equipment as specified in the pre-job hazard assessment. All personal protective equipment and emergency equipment required for use in a confined space will be inspected by a competent person before workers enter the confined space to ensure that the equipment is in good working condition. Written records of the inspections will be retained.

Review

This confined space code of practice will be reviewed on a per job basis or when deemed necessary. All documentation will be kept on file for a period of no less than (3) years
