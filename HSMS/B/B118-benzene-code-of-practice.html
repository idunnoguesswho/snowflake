HSE-COP-007Benzene Code of Practice

Introduction

Benzene is a colourless or light-yellow liquid with a sweet odour and is classified as a known human carcinogen. Exposure to benzene can occur in oil and gas operations, including production, processing, commissioning, maintenance, sampling, and emergency response activities. Both acute and chronic exposure to benzene can result in serious health effects, including damage to the blood-forming system.

This Code of Practice establishes requirements for the identification, assessment, control, monitoring, and management of benzene exposure at DS Home Computing worksites in both Canada and the United States.

Purpose

The purpose of this Code of Practice is to:

Prevent occupational exposure to benzene

Establish consistent requirements for benzene hazard control

Define roles, responsibilities, and procedures for benzene-related work

Ensure monitoring, respiratory protection, training, and medical considerations are addressed

Support compliance with applicable Canadian and U.S. regulatory requirements

Roles & Responsibilities

Management

Implementing and maintaining this Code of Practice

Ensuring benzene hazards are identified and assessed

Providing appropriate engineering, administrative, and PPE controls

Ensuring exposure monitoring is conducted where required

Ensuring workers receive appropriate training

Supporting medical surveillance requirements where applicable

Reviewing benzene-related incidents and corrective actions

Supervisors

Identifying tasks with potential benzene exposure

Ensuring hazard assessments are completed prior to work

Confirming exposure controls are implemented

Ensuring workers use required PPE and respiratory protection

Verifying monitoring equipment is available and functional

Stopping work if unsafe benzene conditions exist

Workers

Participating in required training

Following benzene safe work procedures

Using required PPE and respiratory protection

Reporting potential exposures, spills, or leaks immediately

Leaving the area if unsafe benzene conditions are identified

Scope

This Code of Practice applies to all DS Home Computing employees and worksites in Canada and the United States where benzene may be present, including but not limited to:

Oil and gas production and processing facilities

Commissioning and start-up activities

Sampling, venting, and draining operations

Maintenance and instrumentation work

Confined space entry

Emergency response situations

Terms and Definitions

Benzene:A volatile, flammable, aromatic hydrocarbon classified as a carcinogen.

Action Level:An airborne concentration of benzene that triggers additional exposure controls, monitoring, or medical surveillance.

Occupational Exposure Limit (OEL):The maximum allowable concentration of benzene in workplace air.

Short-Term Exposure Limit (STEL):The maximum allowable concentration over a short-term exposure period.

Medical Surveillance:A program of medical examinations intended to detect health effects related to benzene exposure.

Procedures

Benzene Hazard Identification

Benzene hazards shall be identified prior to work and whenever conditions change.

Benzene may be present during:

Crude oil and condensate handling

Sampling of hydrocarbons

Venting, bleeding, or draining operations

Instrumentation installation and maintenance

Confined space entry

Emergency response activities

Where uncertainty exists, benzene presence shall be assumed.

Exposure Limits and Regulatory Requirements

Benzene exposure limits shall meet the most stringent applicable requirement for the jurisdiction and client.

At a minimum, the following shall apply:

8-hour TWA: 1 ppm

STEL: 5 ppm

Action levels shall trigger:

Increased monitoring

Review of control measures

Additional PPE or respiratory protection

Exposure Monitoring

Exposure monitoring shall be conducted when:

Benzene may be present above background levels

Tasks have potential for inhalation exposure

Required by regulation or client

Monitoring may include:

Personal air sampling

Area monitoring

Monitoring results shall be documented and retained.

Control Measures

Benzene exposure shall be controlled using the hierarchy of controls:

Engineering Controls

Closed systems

Ventilation

Process isolation

Administrative Controls

Work procedures

Permits and task planning

Limiting exposure duration

Personal Protective Equipment

Respiratory protection

Chemical-resistant gloves and clothing

Respiratory Protection

Respiratory protection shall be used when:

Benzene concentrations exceed applicable limits

Exposure levels are unknown

IDLH or unknown atmospheres require:

Pressure-demand SCBA, or

Supplied-air respirator with auxiliary escape air

Air-purifying respirators may only be used where:

Oxygen levels are adequate

Cartridge selection is appropriate

Change-out schedules are established

Respirator use shall comply with the Respiratory Protection Code of Practice.

Skin and Eye Protection

Benzene can be absorbed through the skin.

Chemical-resistant gloves and protective clothing shall be used where contact may occur.

Eye and face protection shall be worn where splashing is possible.

Confined Space Considerations

Confined spaces with potential benzene hazards require:

Atmospheric testing prior to entry

Continuous monitoring during entry

Appropriate respiratory protection

Benzene hazards shall be documented on confined space permits.

Spill and Leak Response

Benzene spills or leaks shall be reported immediately.

Non-essential personnel shall evacuate the area.

Only trained and authorized personnel may respond.

Appropriate PPE and respiratory protection shall be used.

Medical Surveillance

Medical surveillance shall be implemented where required by regulation or exposure levels.

Workers shall be informed of medical surveillance requirements and results.

Training and Competency

Workers with potential benzene exposure shall receive training prior to work.

Training shall include:

Health effects of benzene

Exposure routes

Control measures

PPE and respiratory protection

Emergency response

Refresher training shall be provided as required.

Incident Reporting and Investigation

Benzene-related incidents, exposures, or near misses shall be reported immediately.

Incidents shall be investigated to determine root causes and corrective actions.

Recordkeeping

Records shall be maintained for:

Exposure monitoring

Training

Medical surveillance (where applicable)

Incident investigations

Records shall be retained in accordance with regulatory requirements.

Stop Work Authority

All workers have the authority to stop work if unsafe benzene conditions exist.

Work shall not resume until hazards are reassessed and controls implemented.

Training

Benzene training shall be provided prior to exposure and refreshed as required. Training records shall be maintained.
