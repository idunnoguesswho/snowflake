HSE-POL-1.01General Health & Safety Policy

Introduction

DS HOME COMPUTING is committed to the implementation of a Health, Safety, and Environmental (HSE) System which will protect the physical, psychological, and social well-being of its people, contractors, and communities. This policy demonstrates our dedication to ensuring compliance with all relevant safety regulations and laws, as well as industry best practices.

Our primary objective is to ensure a safe and healthy work environment by preventing workplace accidents, illnesses, and injuries. Through teamwork and accountability, we strive to maintain a culture where safety is everyone's responsibility.

Purpose

This policy outlines DS HOME COMPUTING' commitment to protecting the health, safety, and well-being of all employees, contractors, and stakeholders. It establishes the framework for meeting legal, regulatory, and industry standards and defines the responsibilities at all levels within the organization.

Roles and Responsibilities

Management

Ensure compliance with all relevant U.S. OSHA and Canadian OHS laws, regulations, and standards.

Integrate health and safety considerations into strategic business planning and daily operations.

Provide necessary resources, including training and equipment, to meet safety standards.

Foster a workplace culture that prioritizes the safety and health of employees and contractors.

Conduct regular reviews of safety programs and performance to ensure continual improvement.

Ensure that required corrective actions are taken promptly.

Supervisors

Communicate and enforce safe work practices in alignment with OSHA standards and Canadian regulations.

Ensure that workers have the appropriate training, knowledge, and equipment to work safely.

Monitor work conditions and report hazards, ensuring compliance with all health and safety protocols.

Investigate incidents and take immediate corrective actions to prevent recurrence.

Foster open communication regarding health and safety issues.

Workers

Follow all safety and health policies, procedures, and training requirements as mandated by OSHA and Canadian OHS regulations.

Take personal responsibility for their own safety and that of their co-workers.

Report hazards, unsafe practices, or incidents to supervisors immediately.

Use all provided personal protective equipment (PPE) correctly and report any defects or inadequacies.

Participate in required safety training and programs.

Scope

This policy applies to all DS HOME COMPUTING operations, employees, contractors, and partners across all locations, both in the U.S. and Canada. It is applicable to all work activities and environments, ensuring that safety, health, and environmental standards are upheld and continuously improved.

Terms and Definitions

OSHA: Occupational Safety and Health Administration, the agency responsible for worker safety and health protection in the U.S.

OHS: Occupational Health and Safety, Canadian legislation that mandates workplace safety standards across various provinces.

PPE: Personal Protective Equipment, including helmets, gloves, eye protection, etc., used to minimize exposure to hazards.

Hazard: Any source of potential harm or adverse health effects on individuals.

Incident: An occurrence that results in or has the potential to result in injury, illness, or property damage.

Competent Person: A person who has the knowledge, training, and experience to recognize and address workplace hazards and safety requirements.

Procedures

Hazard Identification and Risk Assessment

Regular workplace inspections are required to identify and assess potential hazards.

Supervisors must ensure that risk assessments are carried out in line with OSHA and OHS requirements.

Appropriate corrective actions must be implemented to mitigate risks.

Incident Reporting and Investigation

All incidents, including near-misses, must be reported immediately to supervisors.

Investigations must follow both OSHA’s incident investigation protocols and Canadian OHS requirements to determine the cause and prevent recurrence.

Safety Inspections

Routine safety inspections will be conducted in compliance with OSHA and OHS regulations.

Any hazards identified during inspections must be addressed immediately, and corrective actions documented.

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Requirements

Workers must wear PPE as required by both OSHA standards and Canadian OHS regulations.

Supervisors must ensure that PPE is inspected regularly and maintained in proper working condition.

Emergency Preparedness and Response

Emergency plans, including fire evacuation and medical emergency response, will be established at each worksite.

Procedures will comply with OSHA’s Emergency Action Plans and relevant Canadian emergency response guidelines.

Training

All employees and contractors are required to complete health and safety training specific to their job roles and in compliance with both OSHA and Canadian OHS regulations.

Training will include, but not be limited to:

Hazard Communication (HAZCOM) and GHS (Globally Harmonized System) standards, WHMIS.

Fall Protection, PPE, and Machine Guarding.

Site-specific safety protocols and emergency response procedures.

References

Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA): 29 CFR 1910 (General Industry), 29 CFR 1926 (Construction).

Canada Occupational Health and Safety Regulations: SOR/86-304, and applicable provincial regulations such as Alberta OHS Act, Regulations and Code, and CSA Standards.

WHMIS: Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System, Canada’s national hazard communication standard.

The commitments contained in this policy statement depend entirely on our people taking ownership of Health, Safety and Environmental performance.  Ultimately, through our joint efforts, we strive to ensure that everyone makes it home safely at the end of each day.
