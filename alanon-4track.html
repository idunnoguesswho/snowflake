How to Use This Workbook

📖 Read Your Own Track

Each session has four tracks. Sit apart for 15 minutes and read only your track. Same themes, different books, different angles. You don't need to read the other tracks to fully participate.

✏️ Write in the Space Provided

Each page has lined writing space for all four tracks. In GoodNotes, write directly on the lines with Apple Pencil. Even a few words counts. Writing surfaces things that thinking alone doesn't reach.

🧒 Track C — Alateen

Written for teens aged 12–18 affected by someone else's drinking. The language is direct and honest. Teens can do this track alone or alongside family members doing their own tracks.

💑 Track D — Recovering Couples (RCA)

Uses the Recovering Couples Anonymous framework — a Twelve Step program for couples healing together. Both partners read Track D together, then reflect together. This track is designed as a "we."

Track A — Al-Anon Member
For the Al-Anon Member
📙 Courage to Change (B-16)Check the index for "detachment" — read that full entry
Detachment doesn't mean you stop caring. Courage to Change describes it as releasing your grip on outcomes — stepping back from the disease without stepping away from the person. We stop absorbing someone else's chaos into our own body and spirit. We allow natural consequences to occur. This is not cruelty. Al-Anon teaches that it is, in fact, the most loving response available to us.
Reflect & Write
Where have you confused detachment with coldness or abandonment?

📖 Courage to Change ▶ Detachment 🏠 Meeting
Track B — Family Member / Friend
For the Family Member / Friend
📙 Courage to Change (B-16) — same entryOn detachment — read slowly
"Detachment with love" sounds like a contradiction. How can you love someone and let go at the same time? Al-Anon teaches that holding on tightly — rescuing, controlling, managing — often removes the natural consequences that might otherwise open a door to change. Letting go is not giving up on the person. It is an act of love for them, and for yourself.
Reflect & Write
What fear surfaces when you imagine stopping your efforts to manage this situation?
What would you do with the energy you currently spend worrying?

📕 From Survival to Recovery (B-21)Introduction — "Growing Up in an Alcoholic Home"
This book describes the survival skills people develop living alongside alcoholism: hypervigilance, people-pleasing, emotional shutdown, and a constant drive to prevent the next crisis. These adaptations kept us safe once. In Al-Anon we begin to ask with honesty: are they still serving us now — or are they quietly running our lives long after the original threat has passed?

Track B — Family Member / Friend
For the Family Member / Friend
📗 How Al-Anon Works (B-22) — "The Family Disease"How alcoholism reshapes everyone in the home
Alcoholism is called a family disease because it does not only affect the drinker. How Al-Anon Works describes how family members develop their own patterns in response: anxiety, walking on eggshells, cancelling plans, hiding the truth, measuring every word. These aren't character flaws or personal failures — they are natural adaptations to an impossible situation that has lasted too long.

📙 Courage to Change (B-16)Check the index for "anger" or "resentment" — read that entry fully
Courage to Change acknowledges anger as a completely natural response to living alongside alcoholism — and also as an obstacle to our own peace when we hold it indefinitely. Anger is often the surface emotion sitting directly over something much softer: fear, grief, love that feels rejected, hope that has been disappointed too many times. For people who love alcoholics, deep anger very often lives right on top of profound love and profound loss.

📗 How Al-Anon Works (B-22) — enabling sectionWhat it is and how it actually works
Enabling isn't weakness. It is not a character flaw. It is love without enough information. How Al-Anon Works explains how protecting someone from consequences — covering for them, minimizing the situation to others, paying debts they created — can remove the very moments that might otherwise open a door to change. This session is not about blame. It is about finally seeing clearly.
