A · Marcus Aurelius — Meditations
"You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength."

Context: Marcus Aurelius wrote this while commanding Roman legions on the Danube frontier — juggling military campaigns, plague, and palace politics simultaneously. He wasn't writing for publication; these were private notes to himself, reminders not to be swept away by things he couldn't control.

For today: On a Friday heading into a summer solstice weekend, there may be things unresolved at work or at home. You can't control what rolls over or what others do — you can only choose your response to it.

Practice: Before reacting to one stressful thing today, pause and ask: "Is this within my control?" If not, release it.
B · AA Daily Reflection — Release from Fear
"The problem of resolving fear has two aspects. We shall have to try for all the freedom from fear that is possible for us to attain. Then we shall need to find both the courage and grace to deal constructively with whatever fears remain." — As Bill Sees It, p. 61

Reflection: Fear used to drive most decisions — and alcohol was one way people tried to silence it. Sobriety doesn't eliminate fear, but the Twelve Steps give a framework for moving through it rather than around it. After five years sober, the author of today's reflection faced a major anxiety — and found that community, the Steps, and spiritual support turned that fear into wholeness and gratitude.

Takeaway: You don't have to be fear-free to move forward. You just need enough courage and grace for today. The rest gets handled one day at a time.
C · Bible Verse of the Day — Proverbs 3:31-32 (NIV)
"Do not envy the violent or choose any of their ways. For the Lord detests the perverse but takes the upright into his confidence."

Context: Proverbs 3 is part of Solomon's wisdom instructions to his son — a guide for navigating a world where corrupt and aggressive people sometimes seem to prosper. The Hebrew word for "confidence" here (sod) means an intimate inner circle or secret counsel. The promise isn't wealth or ease — it's relationship: the upright are brought into God's inner confidence.

Application: When someone pushes ahead by cutting corners or intimidating others, it's easy to wonder if integrity is worth it. This verse says: stay the course. The reward isn't always visible, but it's real.
D · Merriam-Webster Word of the Day
longueur  /lɔ̃ˈɡɜːr/  noun (usually plural: longueurs)

Definition: A tedious, drawn-out passage or section in a work — a book, play, film, or speech — that drags without contributing meaning or energy.

Example: "The documentary was compelling overall, but several longueurs in the middle third tested the audience's patience."

Etymology: Directly from French, where longueur simply means "length." From Latin longus (long). English borrowed it in the 18th century specifically for the aesthetic sense — a stretch that goes on too long to serve its purpose. Unlike the English word "length," it has always carried a slight note of impatience.

F · Britannica Fact
The Summer Solstice isn't actually the longest day everywhere.

At the poles, the solstice is the midpoint of months-long "midnight sun" — the sun never sets from March to September in the High Arctic. But closer to the equator, day length varies only a few minutes year-round. The dramatic "longest day" experience is a mid-latitude phenomenon. Edmonton (53°N) gets roughly 17 hours of daylight today — compared to about 8 hours in December. That 9-hour swing is one of the defining rhythms of Prairie life, tied to planting cycles, energy use, mental health, and even crime rates (which peak in long summer evenings, a phenomenon researchers call "ambient light criminality"). The solstice was celebrated as Midsummer's Night long before Christianity — Stonehenge's alignment with the June sunrise wasn't accidental.
