## A · Marcus Aurelius — Meditations

> "The happiness of your life depends upon the quality of your thoughts."

**Reflection:** Attention shapes experience. The stories we repeat internally become the atmosphere we live in. Marcus wasn't speaking abstractly — he was writing to himself after long days of empire, war, and grief. The point is practical: the quality of your inner narration determines the quality of your day.

**Practice for today:** Replace one recurring negative thought with a deliberate, truthful one.

## B · AA Daily Reflection — Patience

Growth often happens slowly and quietly. Patience with ourselves and others creates room for healing that force never can. Today's reflection is a reminder that we don't have to manufacture progress — we have to stop blocking it. Rushing ourselves or others out of discomfort is its own kind of control. Real change has its own pace.

## D · Merriam-Webster Word of the Day

**emblazon** *(verb)* — im-BLAY-zun

**Definition:** To decorate a surface with a name, slogan, or picture; to display prominently and proudly.

**Example:** Her favourite souvenir was a t-shirt emblazoned with a rosy sunset over the Grand Canyon.

**Etymology:** From French *blazon* (coat of arms), rooted in heraldic tradition. Originally referred to decorating shields and banners with coats of arms; by extension came to mean any vivid, prominent display. *Blazon* itself is a synonym for coat of arms — so to emblazon is literally to coat-of-arms something.

## F · Britannica Encyclopaedia Fact

**Venus: Where a Day Is Longer Than a Year**

Venus rotates so slowly — and in the opposite direction of most planets — that a single Venusian day (243 Earth days) is longer than a Venusian year (225 Earth days). The sun rises in the west and sets in the east. Venus is also the hottest planet in the solar system despite not being the closest to the sun, because its thick CO₂ atmosphere creates a runaway greenhouse effect — surface temperatures hover around 465°C (869°F). It is, in every measurable way, a slow-spinning, backwards-rotating, lead-melting nightmare — and it's Earth's nearest neighbour.
