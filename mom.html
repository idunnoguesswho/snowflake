Module 01
Legal Framework for OHS in Construction
Construction safety in Alberta is governed by the Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHS Act), the OHS Regulation, and the OHS Code. The Act establishes rights and responsibilities; the Regulation sets administrative requirements; the Code specifies technical standards.
  • Prime Contractor: the party responsible for coordinating OHS at a multi-employer worksite. Designated in writing.
  • Employer duties: ensure worker safety, hazard identification, training, supervision, PPE provision.
  • Worker rights: right to know (hazard info), right to participate (OHS committee), right to refuse dangerous work.
  • Due diligence: taking all reasonable precautions — the legal defence for employers against charges.

Module 02
Health & Safety Program Basics
A construction H&S program requires nine core elements:
  • Policy: signed commitment by senior management
  • Roles & responsibilities: clearly defined for all levels
  • Hazard identification & control: formal system (FLHA/JHA)
  • Training & competency: verified, documented
  • Inspections: scheduled and unannounced
  • Incident investigation: near-miss, first-aid, medical-aid, lost-time
  • Emergency response: plan, drills, communication
  • Records & statistics: injury rates, FLHA logs, training records
  • Program review: annual evaluation against leading/lagging indicators

Module 06
Hazard Identification Methods
Five key methods for identifying hazards before work begins:
  • Formal inspections: scheduled walkthroughs by supervisors; documented on inspection forms.
  • Field-Level Hazard Assessment (FLHA): done by workers at the task location before starting work, each shift.
  • Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) / Job Safety Analysis (JSA): step-by-step breakdown of a task to identify hazards at each step.
  • Worker observation and reports: near-miss reporting culture; anonymous reporting systems.
  • Incident trend analysis: looking at past injury/near-miss data to find patterns before the next event.

Module 08
Hierarchy of Controls
Controls ranked from most to least effective:
  • 1. Elimination: remove the hazard entirely (e.g. eliminate working at height by doing work on ground).
  • 2. Substitution: replace with something less hazardous (e.g. water-based paint instead of solvent-based).
  • 3. Engineering controls: physical changes — guards, ventilation, fall arrest anchors, interlocks.
  • 4. Administrative controls: procedures, training, rotation, permits to work, signage.
  • 5. PPE: last line of defence — harness, hard hat, gloves, respirator. Requires fit, training, maintenance.
On construction sites, multiple controls are often used together (defence in depth).

Module 15
WHMIS & Safety Data Sheets (SDS)
WHMIS 2015 is aligned with the Globally Harmonized System (GHS). Key components:
  • Labels: product identifier, pictograms, signal word (DANGER / WARNING), hazard statements, precautionary statements, supplier info.
  • Safety Data Sheets (SDS): 16 standardized sections. Critical sections: Section 2 (Hazard ID), Section 4 (First Aid), Section 7 (Handling/Storage), Section 8 (PPE/Exposure Controls), Section 13 (Disposal), Section 14 (Transport).
  • 9 WHMIS pictograms: flammable, oxidizer, corrosive, acute toxicity, health hazard, environmental hazard, explosive, gases under pressure, biohazard.

Module 10–12
Construction Hazards: Falls, Struck-By, Electrical, Confined Space
The Fatal Four in construction account for the majority of fatalities:
  • Falls: #1 cause. Controls: guardrails, travel restraint, fall arrest, safety nets. Trigger height in Alberta: 3 m (10 ft) on most tasks.
  • Struck-by: falling objects, vehicles, swinging loads. Controls: hard hats, exclusion zones, spotters, tool tethers.
  • Caught-in/between: rotating equipment, trench collapse, pinch points. Controls: lockout/tagout (LOTO), shoring, machine guards.
  • Electrical: overhead lines, buried cables, temporary wiring. Use Dig Safe (Alberta 1-Call), maintain setback distances, GFCIs for temp power.
  • Confined space: permit-required CS requires: atmospheric testing, isolation, rescue plan, entry permit, attendant.

PMP Study Guide 📊
Project Management Professional — PMI PMBOK 7th Edition + Agile Hybrid
Domain 1
People — Leading & Managing Project Teams
The People domain focuses on skills to lead and motivate project teams effectively.
  • Servant leadership: leader removes obstacles for the team rather than directing. Core to Agile.
  • Team development stages (Tuckman): Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning.
  • Conflict resolution techniques (most to least preferred): Collaborate/Problem-solve → Compromise → Accommodate → Avoid → Force/Direct.
  • Motivation theories: Maslow's hierarchy, Herzberg's hygiene/motivators, McGregor's Theory X/Y, McClelland's needs (achievement, affiliation, power).
  • Emotional intelligence: self-awareness, self-regulation, empathy, social skills — essential for stakeholder engagement.

Domain 2
Process — Managing Project Work
The Process domain covers the technical side of managing a project from initiation to close.
  • Project charter: authorizes the project; names the PM; issued by the sponsor.
  • WBS (Work Breakdown Structure): decomposes deliverables into work packages. 100% rule — everything is captured.
  • Critical Path Method (CPM): identifies the longest sequence of dependent tasks. Float = flexibility.
  • Earned Value Management (EVM): SV = EV − PV (schedule variance). CV = EV − AC (cost variance). SPI = EV/PV. CPI = EV/AC. Values >1 = good.
  • Risk management: Identify → Qualitative → Quantitative → Response → Monitor. Responses: avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept (for threats). Exploit, share, enhance, accept (for opportunities).
  • Change control: all scope changes go through formal change control process. No scope creep.

Domain 3
Business Environment — Strategy & Governance
Projects exist within an organizational and strategic context.
  • Business case: justifies the project investment; owned by the sponsor.
  • Benefits realization: PM ensures project delivers promised value; often extends beyond project close.
  • Organizational structures: Functional (PM has little authority) → Matrix (shared authority) → Projectized (PM has full authority).
  • Project governance: PMO, steering committees, stage gates, lessons learned repository.
  • Compliance & ethics: PMI Code of Ethics — responsibility, respect, fairness, honesty. Mandatory and aspirational standards.
  • Tailoring: adapting methodology (predictive, agile, hybrid) to fit the project context.

Agile / Hybrid
Scrum, Kanban & Hybrid Approaches
The PMP exam is ~50% predictive, ~50% agile/hybrid.
  • Scrum roles: Product Owner (backlog, value), Scrum Master (servant leader, removes impediments), Development Team (self-organizes).
  • Scrum events: Sprint Planning → Daily Scrum (standup) → Sprint Review (demo) → Sprint Retrospective (process improvement). Sprint = 1–4 weeks.
  • Kanban: visualize work, limit WIP, manage flow. No fixed iterations — continuous delivery.
  • Agile values (Agile Manifesto): individuals over processes; working software over docs; customer collaboration over contracts; responding to change over following a plan.
  • Hybrid: predictive planning at the portfolio level with agile delivery at the team level. Common in construction tech and enterprise.
  • Velocity: story points completed per sprint. Used for forecasting, not performance evaluation.
